Linguistics glossary

Clear, short definitions of 36 key terms in linguistics, from affix to word order. Type in the box to filter the list instantly.

Morphology

Affix

A bound morpheme attached to a root, such as a prefix (un-) or suffix (-ness).

Morphology

Agglutinative language

A language that builds words by stringing together many morphemes, each with a clear meaning, as in Turkish or Tamil.

Phonology

Allophone

A variant pronunciation of a phoneme that does not change meaning, such as the aspirated and unaspirated “p” in English.

Semantics

Ambiguity

When a word or sentence has more than one possible meaning.

Sociolinguistics

Bilingualism

The ability to use two languages; many people worldwide use two or more languages daily.

Sociolinguistics

Code-switching

Moving between languages or varieties within a conversation or even a sentence.

Historical linguistics

Cognate

Words in different languages that come from a common ancestor, like English “night” and German “Nacht”.

Phonetics

Consonant

A speech sound made with some closure or narrowing in the vocal tract, such as [p], [s] or [m].

Contact linguistics

Creole

A stable, fully developed language that grows from a pidgin and is learned by children as a first language.

General

Descriptive approach

Describing how people actually use language, rather than prescribing how they should.

Sociolinguistics

Dialect

A variety of a language linked to a region or social group, with its own pronunciation, vocabulary and grammar.

Pragmatics

Discourse

Language in use across more than one sentence, such as conversations, stories and texts.

Sociolinguistics

Endangered language

A language at risk of falling out of use, often because fewer children are learning it.

Historical linguistics

Etymology

The history of a word: where it came from and how its form and meaning have changed.

General

Grammar

The system of rules, mostly unconscious, that speakers use to form words and sentences.

Semantics

Homophone

Words that sound the same but differ in meaning, like “flower” and “flour”.

Pragmatics

Implicature

Meaning that is suggested rather than stated directly, worked out from context.

Morphology

Inflection

A morpheme that adds grammatical information, such as tense or number, without creating a new word.

Phonetics

International Phonetic Alphabet

A standard set of symbols, abbreviated IPA, for writing the sounds of any spoken language.

Historical linguistics

Language family

A group of languages descended from a common ancestor language.

General

Lexicon

The mental dictionary of words and morphemes that a speaker knows.

Phonology

Minimal pair

Two words that differ in just one sound, like “ship” and “sheep”, used to identify phonemes.

Morphology

Morpheme

The smallest unit of meaning in a language.

Phonology

Phoneme

The smallest unit of sound that can distinguish meaning in a particular language.

Contact linguistics

Pidgin

A simplified contact language that develops between groups without a shared language.

Pragmatics

Pragmatics

The study of how context, intention and shared knowledge shape meaning.

General

Prescriptive approach

Setting out rules for how people should speak or write, often based on a standard variety.

Phonology

Prosody

The rhythm, stress and intonation of speech, which can signal questions, emphasis or emotion.

Sociolinguistics

Register

A variety of language suited to a particular situation, such as formal, casual or technical.

Semantics

Semantics

The study of meaning in words, phrases and sentences.

General

Sign language

A natural language expressed with the hands, face and body, with its own full grammar.

Phonology

Syllable

A unit of speech organised around a vowel or vowel-like sound, as in the two syllables of “water”.

Syntax

Syntax

The study of how words combine into phrases and sentences.

Phonology

Tone language

A language in which pitch distinguishes word meanings, as in Mandarin or Yoruba.

Phonetics

Vowel

A speech sound made with an open vocal tract, such as [a], [i] or [u].

Syntax

Word order

The typical order of subject, verb and object in a language’s sentences.
