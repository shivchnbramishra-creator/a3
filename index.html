Writing systems

Speech first, writing second

Linguists treat speech and signing as primary: writing is a later invention used for only some languages. Writing systems record language in strikingly different ways.

Alphabets

Letters stand for individual consonants and vowels, as in the Latin, Greek and Cyrillic scripts.

Logographic

Characters represent words or morphemes, as in Chinese characters and Japanese kanji.

Mixed systems

Egyptian hieroglyphs combined signs for sounds with signs for whole words and meanings.

Sign languages

Full natural languages with their own grammar, expressed through handshape, movement and the face.

Ask a question
What is linguistics?

Linguistics is the scientific study of language. Linguists study how speech sounds work, how words and sentences are built, how meaning is created and how languages vary and change across time, place and communities.

Do linguists speak many languages?

Some do, but it isn’t required. Linguistics is about understanding how language works in general, so a linguist might study the grammar of a language they don’t speak fluently.

How many languages are there in the world?

Estimates are usually around 7,000 living languages, though the exact number depends on how languages and dialects are counted. Many are spoken by small communities and are considered endangered.

What is the difference between a language and a dialect?

Linguistically, every dialect is a fully systematic variety. Whether a variety is called a language or a dialect often depends on history, politics and identity as much as on linguistic differences.

Is there a “correct” way to speak?

Linguists describe how people actually use language rather than prescribing rules. Standard varieties are useful in certain settings, but all dialects have consistent grammar.
